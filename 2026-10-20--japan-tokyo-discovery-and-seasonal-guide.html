Two selected seasonal evenings — part of the itinerary

These are the only two new seasonal must-visit choices agreed on 24 October. Their proposed dates organise the trip, but neither the ticket nor the venue requires a fixed time-slot reservation. They do not replace any pre-existing plans or the 19 Nov shopping day.

Saturday 14 Nov — Koishikawa Korakuen night garden (~18:00–19:30)

  • Experience: illuminated Edo-era landscape garden, water reflections/bridges, autumn-themed lighting and projections. Advance tickets cover 13–24 Nov 2026, 18:00–21:00, last admission 20:00; confirm event and ticket details before travel. KKday product/redemption rules · official garden announcement.
  • Route: Koishikawa Korakuen West Gate is the night entrance (not East Gate). Iidabashi Station is a practical rail access point. Arrive around 17:45–18:00; allow 60–90 min. On site, follow the venue's ticket-redemption instructions; do not redeem before arrival.
  • Afterwards (optional, not a third seasonal commitment): Kagurazaka / Hyogo Yokocho for historic alleys and dinner; skip if you prefer an early night. No separate Kagurazaka booking.
  • Flexible-date fallback: If Saturday crowds/weather interfere, move Koishikawa to a free Tokyo evening before 21 Nov; the ticket remains valid to 24 Nov. Don't collide with the 15–17 Nov Hakone block or other fixed plans.

Thursday 19 Nov — Yebisu Garden Place Christmas (~17:30–20:00)

  • Experience: Yebisu Garden Place Center Plaza Baccarat chandelier, Clock Plaza Christmas tree and market, and Garden Place Tower 38F SKY LOUNGE (optional free indoor skyline view). About 2–3 hours if including food or a view; no admission ticket required for outdoor displays.
  • How it fits: Evening after the Shibuya GU/shopping day; particularly convenient if shopping stays in Shibuya. Treat 17:30 arrival as a target, not a rigid constraint on shopping. If the day's shopping ends elsewhere, relocate the Ebisu evening to 18 or 20 Nov after the selected day trip, rather than dropping it.
  • 2026 timing: Baccarat chandelier 14 Nov 2026–11 Jan 2027, typically 11:00–23:00 (only 14 Nov starts 17:00); tree/illuminations from 14 Nov with lights until 23:00; Clock Plaza Christmas market 14 Nov–25 Dec, weekdays 17:00–20:00, weekend 12:00–20:00. The Château Plaza second market does not begin until 27 Nov, after departure. Weather may close the market. Official Christmas programme · Official Baccarat event.
  • Optional afterwards: Ebisu Yokocho for dinner/drinks if there's energy. No need to commit additional districts or special events.

Full-day excursions

Mt Takao — 9.5/10

Why go: Accessible mountain nature, foliage, possible Fuji view. Time: 6–9h door-to-door from Asakusabashi if visiting. Status: Optional only as of 24 Oct; no booking identified. 20 Nov defaults to Yanaka+Nezu or another easy Tokyo day. The 9.5/10 headline above rates scenic attraction, not fit for final full day (rated 8.3/10 for that day). 20 Nov options, effort and optional cable-car/Trail 1 route.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Kiyotaki Station / Takao cable car — Easy mountainside access; the ride itself offers forest views, avoiding the steep first climb. Adult ¥490 one-way / ¥980 return per operator.
  • Takaosan Yakuoin Temple — A distinctive wooded mountain temple along the popular paved Trail 1, with tengu imagery.
  • Mount Takao summit — 599 m panorama and possible Mt Fuji visibility on a clear day; worthwhile lunch/rest destination.

Extras / cautions: Keio Takaosan Onsen Gokurakuyu and Takao 599 Museum near the station. The official Autumn Leaves Festival runs 24 Oct–13 Dec 2026. Foliage and cable-car queues can be strong in November; prefer a weekday and verify last descent.

Kamakura / Enoshima — 9.0/10

Why go: Temples, Enoden coastal train and seaside. Time: Full day if including island. Status: Preferred 18 Nov, weather-flexible, no excursion tickets booked. Full practical Kamakura → Hase → Enoshima guide with return. Rating here is subjective destination fit.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Hasedera Temple (Hase cluster) — Hillside temple, landscaped grounds and Kamakura coastal viewpoints; the strongest blend of temple and nature.
  • Kotoku-in Great Buddha (same Hase cluster) — Impressive outdoor Great Buddha a short walk from Hasedera; easy, worthwhile pairing.
  • Enoshima Island — Seaside exploration and lighthouse/Sea Candle views; separate coastal sub-area, connected by Enoden and footbridge, not next door to Hase.

Extras / cautions: Yoridokoro is the train-side grilled-fish breakfast/lunch you saw (not a beach-view cafe; food sits beside Enoden tracks). Kamakurakōkō-mae Station has famous coast/train scenes and crowds; never stand in the road for photos. Hokokuji Bamboo Temple and Komachi-dori are on the Kamakura Station/east side and add travel time, so treat as an alternate temple-town focus rather than another mandatory coastal stop. Official Enoden 'Noriorikun' one-day pass is ¥800 adult; compare with actual rides before buying.

Tokyo neighbourhoods — select, don't complete them all

Ebisu — 9.4/10

Why go: Romantic lights, free view, izakaya and cocktails. Time: Evening 2–3h. Status: Selected 19 Nov ~17:30–20:00 following the Shibuya Crossing / SKY / shopping day; actual timing flexes around SHIBUYA SKY entry and shopping. Rating is subjective fit.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Yebisu Garden Place + 38F SKY LOUNGE — Free indoor skyline view, not an open-air roof, plus beautiful Garden Place plazas. Baccarat ETERNAL LIGHTS chandelier starts 14 Nov 2026 (11:00–23:00; opening day from 17:00).
  • Ebisu Yokocho — Concentrated covered izakaya alley for dinner, sake and beer; choose a counter on arrival instead of preselecting every restaurant.
  • BAR TRENCH — A small, respected craft-cocktail bar for a quieter, more polished finish; seating and cocktail cost can be limiting.

Extras / cautions: YEBISU BREWERY TOKYO for beer history/taproom and Ebisu Ginza for low-key local streets. Garden Place's 38F viewpoint is genuinely free; it is not a rooftop terrace.

Shinjuku — 9.3/10

Why go: Iconic Japanese drinking alleys and thoughtful shopping. Time: Evening + optional daytime shopping/garden. Status: High interest, not locked. Rating = subjective fit for this trip, not a public review score.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Omoide Yokocho — Lantern-lit miniature grill counters for yakitori and beer: atmospheric, but touristy/crowded.
  • Shinjuku Golden Gai — Tiny characterful bars and music/anime/themed rooms; ask about admission/otōshi and drink prices before sitting.
  • BEAMS JAPAN (Shinjuku flagship) — Several floors of well-designed Japan-focused objects, crafts, lifestyle finds and gifts, matching your shopping style better than anonymous fashion malls.

Extras / cautions: Shinjuku Gyoen for garden time (¥500; generally 9:00–16:30 in November; 1–15 Nov chrysanthemum exhibition); Tokyo Metropolitan Government Observatory for another free indoor high view; Isetan Shinjuku depachika for food gifts; Hands Shinjuku for practical Japanese goods. Cover charge: often ¥500–1,500 each, separate from drinks; cash is prudent, and do not follow street touts.

Shibuya (with optional Harajuku / Omotesando) — 9.2/10

Why go: Shibuya Crossing + SHIBUYA SKY, GU shopping, rooftops and sake. Time: 19 Nov daytime before Ebisu. Status: Crossing and SKY desired; check the two-week advance timed ticket window; Harajuku optional. Practical Shibuya → Ebisu routing and ticket notes.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • GU Shibuya flagship (large store) — Confirmed official GU branch with both men's and women's collections; a deliberate affordable-fashion shopping stop.
  • Shibuya PARCO 10F Rooftop Park — Free outdoor elevated terrace, small rather than panoramic; good pause and city views without a paid observatory.
  • Junmaishu YATA Shibuya — Sake-specialist standing bar. Its own menu advertises a ¥2,000 60-minute sake-tasting course, but recheck price/terms before visiting.

Extras / cautions: SHIBUYA SKY for spectacular paid rooftop (weather-dependent, advance tickets recommended), MIYASHITA PARK rooftop for another free elevated public space, and Nonbei Yokocho for small drinking lanes. Club option, only if interested: WOMB for late-night electronic music (check the official November event schedule; late finish and transit home require separate thought). Meiji Jingu and Omotesando/Harajuku are a different walkable subcluster, not three more required Shibuya stops.

Ueno / Okachimachi — 8.9/10

Why go: Local market energy, easy park, low-key drinking. Time: Half-day or unplanned afternoon/evening. Status: Very convenient from Asakusabashi. Rating = subjective fit for this trip, not a public review score.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Ameyoko Shopping Street — High-energy market between Ueno and Okachimachi, with cheap snacks and a cluster of casual bars/izakayas.
  • Shinobazu Pond / Bentendo — Free, restful park lake with a small island shrine; nice contrast to Ameyoko.
  • Ueno Toshogu Shrine — Historic gilded shrine nestled inside Ueno Park, worthwhile small temple/shrine experience without a day trip.

Extras / cautions: Tokyo National Museum if you want a proper cultural stop; Yanaka Ginza for a quieter, lesser-known historic shopping area farther north (not the same immediate Ueno station cluster). Ueno isn't necessarily peak-red in mid-November; choose it for atmosphere and convenience, not guaranteed foliage.

Roppongi — 9.0/10

Why go: Hotel-near lights, skyline, cocktail nights. Time: Easy evening and/or partial daytime. Status: Strong value because first hotel is here. Rating = subjective fit for this trip, not a public review score.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Roppongi Keyakizaka-dori / Roppongi Hills Christmas — Blue-white Christmas illumination and Tokyo Tower glimpses: confirmed 5 Nov–25 Dec 2026, in walking reach of remm Roppongi.
  • Tokyo City View (Roppongi Hills Mori Tower 52F) — High-quality paid indoor Tokyo skyline viewing, particularly dramatic after dark; not the same as a free rooftop.
  • Tokyo Midtown / Hinokicho Park — Design, food, architecture and a peaceful adjacent park: a different but nearby Roppongi walking pocket.

Extras / cautions: Mori Garden for a small illuminated garden; 21_21 DESIGN SIGHT for design exhibitions; Azabudai Hills for striking architecture/cafes (do not assume its high-floor view is generally free/open). Important: Tokyo Midtown's MIDTOWN CHRISTMAS 2026 is 26 Nov–25 Dec and therefore too late for this trip; go to Roppongi Hills for actual trip-date Christmas lighting.

Asakusa / Kappabashi — 8.8/10

Why go: Traditional Tokyo, temples, retro drinks and useful crafts. Time: Half day + optional evening. Status: Convenient from Asakusabashi; worthwhile extra area. Rating = subjective fit for this trip, not a public review score.

Save these 3 Google Maps anchors (not a required sequence):

  • Senso-ji / Kaminarimon / Nakamise — Classic red gate, old temple and browsing street; iconic but crowded. Streets are atmospheric after shop closing too.
  • Hoppy Street (Asakusa) — Retro casual izakaya alley for beer, Hoppy and homestyle food; aim earlier than Golden Gai hours.
  • Kappabashi Dougu Street — Distinctive practical Japanese kitchenware, ceramics, knives and realistic food samples—more aligned with your useful-goods shopping than generic malls.

Extras / cautions: Sumida Park for riverside Skytree views, and Kamiya Bar for the historic Denki-Bran drinking tradition. Kappabashi shop hours vary and some close Sundays.

Autumn foliage backup pool (not additional commitments): Koishikawa Korakuen, Shinjuku Gyoen, Meiji Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue, Kitanomaru Garden, and Rikugien are possible Tokyo walks but mid-November foliage is not guaranteed (many low-elevation sites peak later). Yoyogi Park and Inokashira Park are pleasant free parks without stronger priority over your existing destinations. Nikko, Nagatoro, Mt Mitake and Lake Okutama are competing excursions with added transit/commitment; do not expand the itinerary automatically.

Useful drinking and food notes: Yakisoba and okonomiyaki are casual dishes worth trying spontaneously; sake is a priority worth trying at a specialist bar. In Golden Gai, a cover charge/otōshi is a per-person seating fee or served appetiser charged in addition to drinks (commonly ¥500–1,500; venue-dependent) — check menus first, bring cash, ask before photos, do not bring outside food/drinks into tiny bars, and do not accept tout-led bar recommendations. A chūhai/cha-wari may involve shochu mixed with tea, but do not rely on ambiguous video-transcribed terms such as "katame" as instructions for drink strength. Golden Gai official etiquette.
